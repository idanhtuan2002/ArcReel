# Film episodes are adapted from the story, never read from it verbatim

Decided 2026-11-09 by the main agent under the sponsor's instruction: the story is a commercial-quality product that may be published on its own; a film made from it goes through adaptation, dramatization and episode planning, and that work must be complete and not weaker than the donors' ("công việc chuyển thể, biên kịch, chia tập phải đầy đủ, không thua kém các donor"). Research: [workflow stage review](../research/2026-11-08-r2-workflow-stage-review.md), [donor stage mechanisms](../research/2026-11-08-r2-donor-stage-mechanisms.md). It refines the host workflow (`lib/workflow_rules.py`) and [ADR-0111](0111-autonomous-production-run-authority.md)'s stage list.

Before this decision a narration episode was a slice of the story read aloud word for word (`lib/prompt_builders_script.py`: voiceover "不改写、不润色、不删减、不补写"); only the picture layer was adapted. The film therefore kept prose pacing: no opening hook, no compression, no screen structure, and its length followed the slice rather than the target. huohuo runs a screenplay-formatting agent between an episode's source and its storyboard (`drama_script_formatter`, called from `drama-episode-pipeline.ts:400`); DramaClaw plans episodes with conflict, cliffhanger, key events and cast. ArcReel's episode planner already cuts the story into arc-complete episodes with verified, gap-free and overlap-free anchors and a target volume derived from duration and speech rate; what was missing is the adaptation of each episode.

1. **The story is never shortened for the film.** STORY's length and runtime are product cost. Faster stories come from stronger writer models.
2. **A new workflow step, `episode_adaptation`, sits between `episode_plan` and `script_plan_content`** for narration and drama projects whose `episode_adaptation` setting is `screen`. Its state is `EPISODE_ADAPTATION`, its action `adapt_episode`, its task `text_episode_adaptation`; the automatic run gains the stage `ADAPTATION` between `EPISODE_PLAN` and `SCRIPT_PLAN`. A project without the setting keeps `verbatim` (today's behaviour), so existing projects and their script plans are untouched.
3. **What an adaptation is.** One structured answer per episode: logline, central conflict, cast, the cliffhanger, and 1–12 scenes. Each scene names its purpose (the turn or reveal it carries), the verbatim source excerpt it adapts, and its lines (`narration`, `dialogue`, `voiceover`, `action`), with speaker and manner for spoken lines. Narration adaptations are a narrator's screen script (hook first, compressed, present and concrete, quoted dialogue read by the narrator); drama adaptations are a scene-headed screenplay where dialogue carries the conflict and narration is replaced by action and sparing voiceover (huohuo's format: `## S01 | setting · place | time`, `Name: (manner) line`). Neither may change what happens or how the episode ends, nor add events.
4. **Mechanical checks, retried with their reasons.** A draft is accepted only when every scene's excerpt is found in the episode source (letters and digits compared, as DramaClaw's verbatim-quote rule), excerpts follow the source order, the first lies in the first 35% and the last in the last 35% of the source (the whole episode and its ending are covered), every speaker is in the cast and every cast name is a registered character or appears in the source (DramaClaw's name attestation), the first spoken line takes at most 6 seconds (the hook), the length (spoken lines; for drama also action lines, estimated at the speech rate) is 80–120% of the episode's target duration (or, without a target, 40–100% of the source's spoken length), drama has 2–8 scenes with no `narration` lines and at most 30% voiceover, narration has no `action` or `voiceover` lines, and nothing is in a CJK script unless the source language is. Up to three attempts; the model sees the previous attempt's failed checks. A draft that never passes fails the task (the run blocks `ADAPTATION_FAILED`).
5. **The adapted text becomes the script's source.** `adaptations/episode_N.txt` replaces `source/episode_N.txt` as the input of script planning, its basis and the workflow target; every existing script-plan rule (verbatim utterances, coverage, staleness) then applies to the adapted script. `adaptations/episode_N.json` keeps the draft, the checks and a basis digest of everything the adaptation read (episode source, ledger entry, the next episode's hook, the previous episode's ending, cast names, target volume, language, prompt version); a different basis makes the adaptation stale and the workflow asks for it again, which in turn makes the script plan stale.
6. **Serial continuity.** The prompt carries the end of the previous episode's source and the next episode's hook from the ledger, so each cliffhanger leads into the next episode without making adaptations depend on each other.

Not done here: a model-graded fidelity review of adaptations (the mechanical checks and QUALITY's whole-film reviewers stand in), character simulation (StoryBox), and lip-synced drama routes (still unqualified).
